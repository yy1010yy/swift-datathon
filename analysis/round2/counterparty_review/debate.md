# Ordinary counterparty recurrence: independent review and final scope

## Independent starting conclusions

C scanned the full source and partitioned the 9,992,994 blank-label records by exact
directed account pair. Before seeing C's conclusions, B independently regrouped all 32
raw partitions, rebuilt strict-predecessor histories and fitted the same candidate
families. This is independent grouping and scoring on a shared filtered extraction;
it is not a second independent raw CSV filter.

Both analyses reach the same initial conclusion: most observed pairs occur once, but a
small tail recurs hundreds or thousands of times. One zero-truncated Poisson, positive
geometric count distribution, or exponential waiting distribution within each party
group cannot reproduce both the singleton mass and the long tail.

## Self-consistency and deciding calculation

`verify.py` reconciles every common count, interval and cohort cell. Exact totals are:

- 9,992,994 blank-label payments;
- 8,258,480 exact directed pairs;
- 7,629,902 singleton pairs and 628,578 recurring pairs;
- 1,734,514 payments with a strictly earlier same-pair timestamp;
- eight later rows tied at their pair's first timestamp.

The units matter. “Recurring pairs” is not the same as “payments in recurring pairs”
or “payments after a pair's first observation.” The identity
8,258,480 + 1,734,514 = 9,992,994 holds because each observed pair contributes one
first-timestamp row and every remaining row has a strict predecessor except that tied
rows still have the pair's earlier distinct timestamp as specified by the method.

B's count-model total-variation values were independently recomputed from C's observed
and expected cells and match in all 24 party×period×model cells. For full-window BB,
the positive-geometric TV error is 0.11322 and zero-truncated-Poisson error 0.12810;
for BC they are 0.12044 and 0.13763. Earlier-fitted exponential waiting models miss
July by TV 0.149–0.161 across the four party groups. These are large structured errors,
not rounding differences.

## Strongest challenge

The failed homogeneous families do not prove pair-specific loyalty or a single business
mechanism. A mixture of sender activity levels, receiver popularity, corridor selection,
calendar schedules, identifier reuse, and durable pair affinity can all create a large
singleton mass plus a long repeat tail. A sender/receiver-degree-preserving permutation
would help distinguish entity activity from pair affinity, but it was not opened because
this EDA phase is stopping after the current investigations.

Observation-window censoring also blocks a causal “payments slowed in July” claim.
First occurrences may have unobserved history before the file, long future gaps are cut
off at the end, and the July cohort differs sharply from the earlier cohort. For example,
only 210,005 of 3,300,701 early BC pairs recur in July, while 1,219,468 BC pairs first
appear in July. The longer July median gaps therefore do not identify a changed waiting
rate. Blank labels mean no recorded anomaly label, not guaranteed ordinary behaviour.

## Final wording

**Ordinary directed-pair recurrence is generated by a strongly heterogeneous process.**
Within every B/C party group, a single observed-pair Poisson/geometric count rule and a
single exponential waiting rule are contradicted by the singleton mass, long recurrence
tail and later-period waiting distribution. The available data do not identify how much
of that heterogeneity comes from customer activity, recipient popularity, corridors or
pair-specific relationships. This is a reviewed negative/partial reconstruction, not a
claim that one named alternative is the hidden generator.

Evidence: C `counterparty_initial.md` and `counterparty_*` tables; B
`counterparty_initial.md`, `counterparty_method.md`, scripts/logs/tables; this directory's
`verification.json` and recomputed TV table.
